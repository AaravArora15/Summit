00.

What v3 adds, and how it stays legible

Eleven features landed on top of v2. The risk was never building them — it was ending up with five competing colour scales on one card.

The four scales, and how each one is encoded

  • Rarity — the badge palette: sage, slate, rust, near-black. Appears on shields, pins and rarity chips only.
  • Safety — three solid semantic colours: #2E7D4F open, #C08327 changing, #B23A2B closed. Appears nowhere else in the app.
  • Difficulty — new in v3, and deliberately not a colour at all. One to four peaks, drawn in the ink colour of whatever surface they sit on: Gentle, Moderate, Hard, Severe. A shape scale can sit beside a rarity chip and a safety chip on the same row without any of the three being misread, and it survives colour-blindness and greyscale screenshots.
  • Set identity — a six-colour "title deed" palette used only on set surfaces: the deed cap, the set stripe, the sets board. Never on a pin, never on a shield, never on a chip. This is the Monopoly cue, quarantined to the one place it means something.

Two currencies, and why they must not merge

  • XP is permanent. It only goes up, it drives Explorer Level, and nothing ever takes it away. It answers "who am I".
  • Points are monthly. They come from distance, moving time, new routes, personal records, secret spots and first discoveries, and they reset on the 1st into a fresh league. They answer "how is this month going".
  • A reset that costs nothing. When points reset, badges, serials, sets, titles, XP and level are untouched. The leaderboard is the only thing that starts again — stated in plain words on the league screen, because a competitive system that appears to delete your collection is a system people quit.
  • Points are visible per activity. Screen 12 breaks every point down by source. Nothing is awarded by a number the climber cannot trace back to something they actually did.

Pioneer numbering — scarcity with a floor under it

  • The format is MACRITCHIE · PIONEER #0047. Location code in caps, then the serial. It reads as a place you went, not as checkpoint #42, and it is the string people screenshot.
  • Every location has a hard cap — 500 pioneer numbers for a launch city, tuned to installs so the last one is genuinely reachable. The cap is printed on the location, with a meter, before you walk: 47 / 500 claimed.
  • Past the cap, nothing is lost. Number 501 gets the same badge in its Explorer edition, without a serial. The scarcity is on the number, never on the badge, the set, the points or the trek. That is what stops a capped system from reading as "you are too late for this app".
  • Caps scale with the city. One field on the location, set per launch region. A 500-cap in Singapore and a 5,000-cap in a market ten times the size behave identically to the climber.

What changed from v2

  • The journal now carries trek data. v2 deliberately hid distance and pace. Points are earned from distance, moving time and personal records, so hiding the inputs would have made the scoring unauditable. Place and serial still lead every entry; the numbers sit underneath.
  • The fourth tab is League, not Party. Crews still exist, and now live inside League alongside the monthly board and private group competitions — one social destination instead of two.
  • Collection opens on a dashboard. The flat badge grid became screen 13, one tap deeper. The tab now opens on totals, set progress and the set closest to finishing.
  • Mystery became weekly. v2's mystery was ambient and permanent. It is now a Monday drop with three clues released across the week, which gives the feature a heartbeat and a reason to open the app on a Wednesday.
  • The inactivity nudge is the only push we added, and it is written as an invitation with a fact in it — never a streak, never a loss, never a countdown.

Navigation & flow

  • Five tabs. Home · Collect · Explore · League · Profile, with Explore in the raised centre slot. League replaces v2's Party tab and contains three segments: the monthly board, your crew, and group competitions.
  • Collect opens on the dashboard (13), with the sets board (14), badge grid, mystery archive (18) and titles (19) one level beneath it.
  • The trek loop runs 05 → 06 → 07 → 09 → (10) → 11 → 12, and every exit from 12 lands on a visible gap: a league position, a set at 4 / 5, or a group competition you are second in.
  • The mystery loop runs 05 → 08 → the map, with no route and no pin. It is the only flow in the app that deliberately withholds navigation.
  • Tabs hide on every full-screen moment: arrival, reward, set complete, mystery, paywall.
  • Safety interrupts 31 is reachable from 07 and 09; 32 can appear at any point and always offers a named open alternative.

Points & league rules

  • Nine sources, all published in-app on screen 20: 10/km, 1/min moving, +150 new route, +200 personal record, +250 under community average, +250 secret spot, +300 first discovery, +400 set complete, +500 submission goes live.
  • Bonuses are flat and one-off. Nothing scales with speed, so two people covering the same ground score the same regardless of pace.
  • Five tiers — Trailhead, Foothills, Ridgeline, Highlands, Summit — in divisions of 42. Top eight promote, bottom eight relegate, everyone else holds.
  • The reset touches points only. Badges, serials, sets, titles, XP, level, spots and submissions carry over untouched, and the screen says so.
  • Group competitions are isolated from the league by design, and stated as such on the create screen, so starting one carries no ranking risk.
  • Opting out of the league keeps points accruing privately. Competition is a feature, not a condition of use.

Mock data, structured for real APIs

  • Eight entities carry every screen: Explorer, Place, Badge, Set, SecretSpot, Trek, Competition, Season. Discovery is the join: { explorerId, placeId, badgeId, serial, claimedAt, trekId }.
  • The serial is issued by the server, once. place.serialCap is a field, not a constant, so a launch city can cap at 500 and a larger market at 5,000 with no client change. When issued >= cap, the badge issues with serial: null and renders as the Explorer edition.
  • The unknown is a field, not a screen. revealState: 'mystery' | 'clued' | 'known' | 'claimed', and every surface renders from that one value.
  • Points are computed from a Trek, never written ad hoc: scoreTrek(trek, context) → LineItem[], and screen 12 renders the line items directly. The rate table on screen 20 is the same object, so the published rates cannot drift from the awarded ones.
  • Safety is its own object on the place — { status, weather, trailStatus, terrain, difficulty, duration, access } — so a real NParks or weather feed swaps in behind one interface, and status: 'closed' routes to screen 32 from anywhere.
  • Submissions have a state machine: received → in_review → live | declined, with a decline reason string that is always rendered to the submitter.
  • Everything sits behind a repository layer returning promises, so replacing mock data with a backend touches no component.

How this maps to RevenueCat

  • One entitlement, "pro". Every gate reads a single boolean from CustomerInfo.entitlements.active["pro"]. No per-feature SKUs.
  • One offering, two packages. $rc_annual (default, pre-selected) and $rc_monthly. Prices, currency and the trial badge on screen 35 come from the fetched Offering, so a Singapore price renders in SGD without a build.
  • Five gated surfaces, all marked in the mock: the four advanced map filters, journal history beyond 90 days, more than one active group competition, featured slots five to eight, and offline maps with GPX export. Each opens the same sheet from screen 34.
  • Restore purchases appears twice, as required: in the paywall header and under Settings → Subscription.
  • Never gated: claiming a place, pioneer numbers, badges, sets, titles, points, the monthly league, the weekly mystery, secret spots, submissions, crews, and every safety and weather feature. Locking any of those would turn a game into a toll road.

Decisions I need from you before the build

  • The pioneer cap number. 500 per place is drawn throughout. It should be roughly one fifth of expected installs in the launch city — high enough that a keen climber can still take a number in month six, low enough that the number means something. Give me the install estimate and I will set it.
  • Points versus XP. The mock runs both: XP for permanent level, points for the monthly league. It is defensible and it is also two numbers to explain. If you want one, points can drive level too — but then a monthly reset needs a lifetime total sitting beside it, and screens 12, 20, 21 and 26 change.
  • Secret spot seeding. The mock assumes two to three pre-placed spots per major route, authored by us, plus user submissions on top. Somebody has to walk those routes and place them — that is real fieldwork before launch, and it is the single biggest content cost in v3.
  • Submission review. Three days is printed on screen 28. At launch volume that is one person for an hour a week; it does not stay that way. Decide now whether trusted climbers (say, three live submissions) can approve others.
  • Target platform. Next.js in a phone frame demos fastest and shares by link; Expo gives real GPS, background tracking and push, all of which v3 now genuinely needs. The screens are identical either way; the component code is not.
  • Photography. Screens 10, 16 and 27 fill themselves from climber photos, which is why the illustration stand-in is acceptable for the mockup. Screens 01 and 11 still want real landscape photography and would be raised by it.
  • Currency. Shown as S$6.99 / S$49.99. RevenueCat renders the store's localised price, so these are placeholders for layout only.

How the specsheet became an app

Every rule below comes from the brand sheet. Where the sheet was silent, the choice is noted as an extension rather than a given.

Taken directly from the sheet

  • Outfit, one family. Bold and SemiBold for headings, Medium for subheadings and labels, Regular for body. No second face and no monospace — numbers use Outfit SemiBold with tabular figures, which is what gives the data its own voice.
  • Forest #1F3D2E is the only primary. It carries buttons, the active tab, progress fill and the map's own trail lines. Sand, rust and slate never act as the primary action.
  • Pill buttons with a chevron, exactly as drawn: filled forest primary, white outlined secondary.
  • Shield badges with a name band, replacing the hexagons of v1. Four tiers matched to the sheet's own four badge colours.
  • Soft topographic map in sage with contour lines, a clear trail and floating circular controls.
  • Dark forest and near-black cards for leaderboards and stat blocks, as shown in section 09 of the sheet.
  • Flat, atmospheric illustration — layered ridgelines, a sand-coloured sun, a winding trail — used for every hero rather than stock photography.

Extensions the sheet didn't cover

  • A safety scale. Conditions need three states that cannot be mistaken for rarity, so they use solid semantic colours drawn from the brand's earthy range: #2E7D4F open, #C08327 changing, #B23A2B closed. Rarity stays on the badge palette.
  • A mystery state. The sheet has no "unknown" treatment, so unknowns use a hatched neutral fill and a ? glyph — deliberately the one place in the app with no brand colour at all.
  • Tab bar hierarchy. Explore takes the raised centre position in forest green; the sheet specifies no navigation.
  • Photography. The sheet asks for real, inspirational landscape photography. This mockup ships illustration instead so it stays self-contained and licence-free — screens 01, 10 and 24 are where real photography would replace it.

How this maps to RevenueCat

  • One entitlement, "pro". Every gate in the prototype reads a single boolean from CustomerInfo.entitlements.active["pro"]. No per-feature SKUs.
  • One offering, two packages. $rc_annual (default, pre-selected) and $rc_monthly. Prices, currency and the trial badge on screen 30 come from the fetched Offering rather than hardcoded strings, so a Singapore price renders in SGD without a build.
  • Four gated surfaces, marked in the mock: rarity and terrain filters (Explore), journal entries older than 30 days (Collection), the fourth-and-later mystery reveal each day (Home), and profile frames and featured slots (Profile). Each one opens the same gate sheet from screen 29.
  • Restore purchases appears twice, as required: in the paywall header and under Settings → Subscription.
  • Never gated: claiming a summit, badge serials, safety and weather data, party membership, quest participation, seasonal territory. Locking any of those would turn a game into a toll road.
07.

System spec

The rules the thirty screens are built from, traced back to the specsheet, and what needs deciding before the clickable build starts.

Bottom navigation

  • Home · Collection · Explore · Party · Profile. Explore sits in the centre slot with a raised forest orb, because it must be visually dominant and slot three is the only position that can carry that. The written brief put Explore second; centring it is the one deliberate deviation.
  • Icons come from section 04 of the sheet — mountain, badge shield, compass, flag, person — at 1.7px stroke, no fills.
  • Tabs are hidden on every full-screen moment: arrival, reward, collection complete, paywall, and the mystery flow. The loop's dramatic beats own the whole display.
  • No badge counts on the Collection tab. A rust dot on the avatar signals party and season activity only.

Screen flow

  • Onboarding 01 → 02 → 03 → 04 → Home, once, never repeated.
  • Core loop Home 05 → Explore 06 → sheet 07 → mystery 08 → route 09 → arrival 10 → reward 11 → collection 12. Every exit from 11 lands somewhere with a visible gap.
  • Collection branch 12 → 13 → 14 on completion → 17 titles; 12 → 15 badge detail; 12 → 16 mystery badge → back to the map.
  • Social branch 19 party → 20 quest; 19 → 21 territory → 22 leaderboard; 23 profile → 17, 18, 28.
  • Safety interrupt 26 is reachable from 07 and 09, and escalates to 27 when a checkpoint locks. 27 can appear at any point in the loop.

  • Bold 700, tracking −2.2%: screen titles, badge names, the numbers that carry drama. Never below 13px.
  • SemiBold 600: card headings, button labels, every figure. All numerals are set with tabular-nums, which is what lets a 5 / 7 sit still while it animates.
  • Medium 500, uppercase, tracking +13%: labels, meta rows, timestamps. This is the app's data voice — the sheet has no monospace, so the small caps label does that job instead.
  • Regular 400: all sentences. Body copy is 14px on device, 15.5px for lead paragraphs.
  • The 4 / 7 pattern is always SemiBold with the achieved half in forest and the total in dim grey. It appears on nine screens and must look identical on all of them.

Motion — smooth, natural, subtle, purposeful

  • Badge reveal (11): the shield scales 0.7 → 1.0 on a natural ease with a small overshoot, then the name band drops in. The bonus card lands 600ms later so the two rewards read as separate events.
  • Counters tick rather than swap. 5 / 7 animates the fifth dot filling.
  • Mystery pulse a 2.8s breathing halo on unknown pins and the ??? block. The only ambient loop in the app.
  • Arrival the ridgelines parallax apart by 12px as the sheet rises, then the shield settles across the seam. No confetti — the place is the reward.
  • All of it respects prefers-reduced-motion; reveals become cross-fades.

Mock data, structured for real APIs

  • Six entities carry every screen: Explorer, Checkpoint, Badge, Collection, Party, Season. Discovery is the join: { explorerId, checkpointId, badgeId, serial, ordinal, claimedAt }.
  • The unknown is a field, not a screen. A checkpoint carries revealState: 'unknown' | 'clued' | 'known' | 'claimed', and every surface renders from that one value. Nothing about mystery is hardcoded per screen.
  • Safety is its own object on the checkpoint — { status, weather, trailStatus, terrain, difficulty, duration, accessibility, access } — so a real NParks or weather feed swaps in behind one interface, and status: 'unavailable' routes to screen 27 from anywhere.
  • Everything sits behind a repository layer (lib/data/*.ts) returning promises, so replacing mock with a real backend touches no component.
  • The bonus roll is a pure function with declared odds in a table, not a random number scattered in the reward component. The odds are printed on screen 11 because that table is the single source of truth.

What changed from v1

  • Light-native, not dark-native. v1 was a dark game UI; the specsheet is cream and forest, so the whole app inverted. Dark surfaces are now reserved for emphasis — the mystery hero, leaderboard leader, quest progress, equipped title — exactly as the sheet uses them.
  • One typeface instead of three. Archivo, Instrument Sans and Martian Mono are gone. Outfit does all four roles, and the mono data voice became uppercase Medium labels with tabular figures.
  • Shields replace hexagons. Badge tiers now match the sheet's own four: sage, slate, rust, near-black with sand.
  • Line icons replace emoji. Every icon in the app is drawn to the sheet's iconography rule.
  • Ember gold is gone. Forest is the only primary; sand only appears on dark surfaces, where the sheet uses it.
  • Vocabulary follows the brand. Discoveries you have claimed are summits; the app speaks to climbers.
